Marking up a PDF is how documents get reviewed. A contract comes back with clauses circled, a draft returns dense with margin notes, a study handout ends up striped with highlighter. Done well, annotation makes a document *clearer*. Done carelessly, it turns a clean page into visual noise. This guide is about doing it well, entirely online, with nothing to install.

## The core markup tools, and when each earns its place

Every annotation tool has a job. Reaching for the right one keeps your markup readable.

- **Highlighter**, for *emphasis on existing text*. Swipe it over the phrase that matters. Keep it translucent so the words show through.
- **Pen (freehand)**: for *organic marks*: a signature, a circle around a number, an arrow, a tick.
- **Rectangle**, for *framing or covering*. Outline a figure to draw attention, or fill one solid to mask an area.
- **Text box**, for *your own words*: a comment, a correction, a value on a form line.
- **Stamp**, for *reusable images*: a signature, a logo, an "APPROVED" graphic.

## Rules for markup that people can actually read

### 1. Pick a colour system and stick to it

Colour carries meaning when it's consistent. A simple scheme:

- **Yellow highlight**. "read this."
- **Red pen/text**: "problem here."
- **Blue text**, "suggestion or question."

Three colours is plenty. More than that and the reader has to decode your palette instead of your comments.

### 2. Highlight sparingly

If half the page is yellow, nothing stands out. Highlight the *key clause*, not the whole paragraph around it. A good rule: if you've highlighted more than a fifth of a page, you're underlining a book, not marking it up.

### 3. Comment near what you mean

Drop text boxes *beside* the thing they refer to, not at the top of the page. Proximity does the work of a leader line. If space is tight, a short arrow from the note to the target keeps the link obvious.

### 4. Keep annotations legible at 100%

It's tempting to zoom in and write tiny. Remember the reader may see the page at full size. Size your text boxes so they're comfortable to read without zooming.

## Reviewing someone else's document

When you're the reviewer, structure helps the author act on your notes:

1. **Number your comments** if there are many, so they can be discussed ("point 3 on page 2").
2. **Separate "must fix" from "nice to have"** using your colour system.
3. **Box or highlight the exact span** you're referring to, vague notes create rework.
4. **Sign or initial** with a stamp if the markup is an approval, so it's clear who signed off.

## Marking up study material

Students annotate differently. The goal is recall, not revision requests:

- Highlight **definitions and dates** in one colour, **examples** in another.
- Use the pen to **connect related ideas** across a page with a quick line.
- Add margin text boxes that **summarise** a paragraph in your own words. The act of rephrasing is half the learning.

## What flattening means for your marks

While you work, your highlights and notes live on a layer above the page, which is why you can nudge and delete them freely. Export is the commitment point: the marks are drawn into the page itself, becoming ordinary page content that any reader displays. Two things follow. Nobody needs a special app to see your annotations, and nobody can silently lift them off either, because after flattening there is no separate annotation object left to remove. The pages you never touched are copied over exactly as they were.
