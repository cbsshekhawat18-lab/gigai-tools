Sometimes a PDF is almost perfect. One page just needs to jump ahead, or a stray sheet has to slide to the back. You don't want to rebuild the document or reorganize every section. You just need to *move a page* and be done. How to do it to do exactly that, quickly.

## Moving one page, the simple way

The whole task comes down to three small steps:

1. **Open the PDF** so its pages appear as thumbnails.
2. **Grab the page** you want to move.
3. **Drop it** in its new position, the pages around it shift automatically.

That's it. You don't touch the pages that are already correct. They hold their places while the one you're moving finds its new home. The numbering updates the moment you let go, so you get instant confirmation the page landed where you intended.

## Drag, or use the arrows

Dragging is great for big jumps: sending page 20 up to position 3, say. But when a page only needs to shift a slot or two, the keyboard is often faster and more precise:

- **Focus the page** (click it or tab to it).
- Press **Alt + Left** to move it one position earlier.
- Press **Alt + Right** to move it one position later.

Tap the arrow a couple of times and the page walks to its spot. No aiming for a drop target, no overshooting. It's also the accessible route, so the tool works fully without a mouse.

## Common single-page moves

A handful of tweaks come up again and again:

- **Bring the cover to the front.** After a merge, the title page sometimes ends up buried, one drag fixes it.
- **Send an appendix to the back.** Reference material that got captured mid-document slides neatly to the end.
- **Promote the summary.** Move an executive summary ahead of the detail so skim-readers hit the point first.
- **Fix a swapped pair.** Two pages in the wrong order? Move one past the other and you're done.

Each of these is a matter of seconds when you're dragging thumbnails rather than juggling page numbers in your head.

## Moving several pages at once

Need to relocate a small run of pages, say, three that belong together? Move them one at a time, in order, to the same destination. Drop the first where you want the block to start, then drop each following page just after it. Because the preview renumbers live, you can watch the block reassemble in the right spot.

For larger reshuffles across a whole document, it's worth stepping back and planning the full sequence rather than moving pages piecemeal, but for a handful of pages, one-by-one is the quickest path.

## Save the move properly

One thing to watch: moving a page on screen and *saving* that move aren't the same. Some viewers only rearrange the display and leave the file untouched, so reopening it brings the old order back.

To make the change permanent, export a rebuilt PDF and keep that downloaded copy. Open it to confirm the page is where you left it before you send the file on. A quick check now saves an awkward "wrong version" email later.

Before anything is written, the tool checks that your new arrangement contains every page exactly once. No silent drops, no accidental duplicates: if the sequence does not account for the whole document, the export refuses rather than guessing. Once the order passes that check, the pages are copied into a fresh document in your chosen sequence, on your device, with no re-rendering step anywhere in the pipeline.
