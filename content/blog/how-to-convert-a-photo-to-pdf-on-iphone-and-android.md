Someone needs a signed form, a receipt, or a screenshot "as a PDF," and you're standing in a queue holding only your phone. The image is already in your camera roll. The missing step is wrapping it in a PDF the other side can open, print, and file. In practice, both iPhone and Android can do this without a single app install, and there's one browser method that works identically on both. How to do it to convert a photo to PDF on your phone, whichever one you carry.

## Why turn a photo into a PDF at all?

A raw `IMG_4821.jpg` is fine for a group chat, but it's the wrong container for anything official. Turning it into a PDF gives you:

- **One tidy file** that opens the same way on every device, instead of a loose image.
- **A print-ready page** with predictable margins, email systems and printers treat PDFs as documents, not media.
- **Multiple photos in order**, locked into a single attachment that can't get shuffled or separated.
- **A "document" feel** that reads as a scan or form rather than a snapshot.

If you only need to email one page, any of the methods below will do. If you need several photos combined into a numbered set, jump to the [combining section](#combining-several-photos-into-one-pdf).

## The method that works on both phones

Before the device-specific tricks, here's the one route that behaves the same on an iPhone, a Pixel, a Samsung, or any other phone: your browser. The [JPG to PDF converter](/jpg-to-pdf) is a web page, so it runs in Safari, Chrome, or whatever browser you already have open. No app store, no account.

1. **Open the tool** in your mobile browser.
2. **Tap the upload area.** Your phone shows its usual picker. Choose the photo from your camera roll, Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or Downloads.
3. **Add more photos if you need them**, then drag the rows to set the page order.
4. **Convert and save** the finished PDF straight back to your phone.

Everything runs on your device, the images never leave your phone, which is the whole point when the "photo" is a payslip or an ID. Because it's identical across platforms, it's the method to remember if you switch phones or help someone with a different one. Now for the built-in shortcuts each system offers.

## Convert a photo to PDF on iPhone

IOS has a native trick hidden in the share sheet, and it's genuinely quick for a single image.

### The Photos share-sheet method

1. Open **Photos** and select the image (or images).
2. Tap **Share** (the square with an arrow).
3. Choose **Print**.
4. On the print preview, **pinch outward** on the page thumbnail with two fingers. This zoom gesture converts the preview into a PDF.
5. Tap **Share** again and choose **Save to Files** or send it on.

It feels like a hidden gesture because it is, but it works reliably and needs nothing installed.

### The Files-app method

If the photo already lives in the **Files** app, long-press it and choose **Create PDF**. iOS builds a PDF next to the original in the same folder instantly. This is the cleanest route when your image is a download or a saved attachment rather than a camera-roll photo.

## Convert a photo to PDF on Android

Android is a little more fragmented, the exact wording depends on your phone maker, but there are two dependable paths.

### The Print-to-PDF method

Android's system print dialog can save to PDF from almost any app:

1. Open the photo in **Google Photos** (or your gallery app).
2. Tap the menu and choose **Print**.
3. At the top of the print preview, change the printer dropdown to **Save as PDF**.
4. Tap the **Save** (download) button and pick a location.

Because this hangs off the OS print system, it works from browsers, gallery apps, and document viewers alike.

### Files by Google

If you use the **Files by Google** app, some versions let you select images and export them straight to PDF from the selection menu. Availability varies by device and app version, which is exactly why the browser method above is a reliable fallback.

## Combining several photos into one PDF

Native share-sheet methods get clumsy the moment you have more than one or two images. You can't easily control which page comes first. This is where a dedicated converter earns its place.

Open the [JPG to PDF converter](/jpg-to-pdf), add every photo (JPG and PNG can mix), then **drag the rows** until the sequence is right. The number on each row is the page it will become. Convert once and you get a single, correctly ordered PDF. If you've already made separate PDFs and want to glue them together, the [Merge PDF tool](/merge-pdf) joins them by touch, letting you drag each file into position before you download the combined document.

## Keep the file small enough to email

Phone photos are large. Three or four high-resolution images can push a PDF past the 25 MB limit that many email providers enforce. If your file bounces or crawls to upload, run it through the [compress PDF tool](/compress-pdf). It shrinks the file on your device while keeping the pages readable. For a receipt, form, or screenshot, you can usually cut the size dramatically with no visible loss.

## Reversing it: pulling images back out of a PDF

Sometimes you need the round trip. If a colleague sends a PDF and you want the individual pictures (to reuse a photo, crop a single page, or re-share one image) the [PDF to JPG converter](/pdf-to-jpg) turns each page back into a saved image on your phone. It's the mirror image of everything above and handy when someone sends a document but you only need one page as a picture.

## Quick tips for a cleaner result

- **Crop before you convert.** Trim the desk or background out of the shot in your phone's photo editor so the page fills the frame.
- **Shoot straight down** for anything document-like, so text lines stay horizontal and edges stay rectangular.
- **Check the order** before converting a multi-photo batch. Reordering after the fact means starting over.
- **Compress last, not first**, so you only shrink the final combined file once.

Every modern phone can turn a photo into a PDF for free. On an iPhone, the Share → Print → pinch gesture (or a long-press **Create PDF** in Files) is fastest for one image. On Android, the **Save as PDF** print option does the same job. But the moment you need a specific page order, several photos in one file, or a method you don't have to relearn on the next device, do it in your browser: [convert your photos to a PDF](/jpg-to-pdf), [merge separate PDFs](/merge-pdf) into one, [compress the result](/compress-pdf) so it emails cleanly, and, if you ever need to go the other way, [turn a PDF back into images](/pdf-to-jpg). Nothing uploads, nothing is stored, and there's nothing to install. Open the tool and you're done in under a minute.

The phone gestures above are perfect for one photo.
