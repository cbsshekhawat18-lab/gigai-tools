Every platform wants a slightly different rectangle, and getting it wrong means a blurry upload, an awkward crop, or a face that's been chopped in half by a banner's "safe zone." This is the 2026 cheat sheet: the exact pixel dimensions worth memorising, and a fast way to hit them.

A note on where these numbers come from: platforms move their goalposts quietly and their official help pages often lag the actual rendering behaviour. ## Instagram

Instagram is the most format-hungry platform, so start here.

- **Square post:** 1080 × 1080 (1:1)
- **Portrait post:** 1080 × 1350 (4:5). Takes up the most feed real estate
- **Story & Reel:** 1080 × 1920 (9:16)
- **Profile photo:** 320 × 320 (displayed as a circle, so keep faces centred)

Instagram compresses uploads hard. Sending a clean 1080-wide image looks far better than uploading a 4000px original and letting the app crush it. Every Instagram surface is also covered in the [image size cheat sheet](/image-size-cheat-sheet).

## YouTube

- **Thumbnail:** 1280 × 720 (16:9), the single most important image on the platform
- **Channel banner:** 2560 × 1440, but the "safe area" that shows on every device is only the central 1546 × 423
- **Video (upload):** 1920 × 1080 for standard 1080p

Keep your thumbnail's key text inside the middle of the frame. The bottom-right corner is covered by the duration stamp. Banner safe zones are mapped in detail on the [YouTube banner page](/for/resize-image-for-youtube-banner).

## Facebook

- **Shared post image:** 1200 × 630 (also the ideal link-preview size)
- **Cover photo:** 820 × 312 on desktop, cropped narrower on mobile
- **Profile photo:** 170 × 170

## X (Twitter)

- **In-stream image:** 1600 × 900 (16:9) displays cleanly without cropping
- **Header:** 1500 × 500 (3:1)
- **Profile photo:** 400 × 400

## LinkedIn

- **Shared post image:** 1200 × 627
- **Personal cover:** 1584 × 396
- **Company logo:** 300 × 300

## TikTok & Pinterest

- **TikTok video / cover:** 1080 × 1920 (9:16), same as a Reel, see [TikTok specs](/for/resize-image-for-tiktok)
- **Pinterest standard pin:** 1000 × 1500 (2:3), tall pins win more space. See [Pinterest specs](/for/resize-image-for-pinterest)

## A few rules that beat any chart

1. **Match the aspect ratio first, pixels second.** If the ratio is right, the platform's downscale is graceful. If it's wrong, you get letterboxing or a bad crop.
2. **Design bigger, export to spec.** Work at 2× the target and resize down at the end for the sharpest result.
3. **Mind the safe zones.** Banners and covers crop differently on mobile and desktop: keep text and faces near the centre.
4. **Export at the right pixel size, not a giant original.** A correctly sized 1080px image usually looks better after the platform's compression than an oversized one.
